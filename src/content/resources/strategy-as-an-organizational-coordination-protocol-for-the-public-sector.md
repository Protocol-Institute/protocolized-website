description: "Vaughn Tan presents a prototype session from a new executive-education course on public sector strategy, arguing that strategy should be redefined as 'making reasoned subjective arguments about acceptable trade-off configurations' rather than planning or objective-setting, framing it explicitly as a coordination protocol for decentralized hierarchies. He critiques how public sector strategy has inherited mid-20th-century American private-sector frameworks (business policy model, SWOT, NPM) ill-suited to public contexts, illustrates coordination failures via Grenfell Tower and desert-city flooding, and introduces the Knightian risk/uncertainty distinction (using WHO's COVID travel-restriction guidance) to argue public servants mismanage uncertainty by treating it as calculable risk. He also proposes 'parametric cases'—short, highly localized, content-specific teaching cases—as a pedagogical fix for traditional long, decontextualized business-school case studies."
