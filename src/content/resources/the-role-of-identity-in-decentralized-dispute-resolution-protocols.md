description: "William George, a mathematician working on mechanism design for the Kleros decentralized dispute resolution protocol, explains how the absence of robust identity tools historically forced protocols like Kleros v1 into purely token-weighted, financialized juror-selection mechanisms vulnerable to Sybil attacks via address splitting. He shows how combining imperfect proof-of-personhood systems (like Proof of Humanity) with financial staking creates a 'Swiss cheese' security model where both layers must be broken simultaneously, and extends this to soulbound tokens (from Weyl/Ohlhaver/Buterin) for credential-filtered, diversity-weighted juror panels inspired by quadratic funding. In Q&A, Venkatesh Rao pushes back that such mechanisms impose an esoteric 'homo economicus' framing that clashes with how real communities handle trust and cheater-detection socially."
