description: "Trent van Epps (Protocol Guild) presents a four-corner political economy framework for protocols adapted from Lawrence Lessig's 'pathetic dot' theory—norms, market, state, and new nature—arguing that protocols generate far more value than they capture and whoever governs the funding/labor/legitimacy flows shapes the protocol itself. He proposes measurable indicators for each corner (e.g., open-source status, corporate sponsorship share, legal mandates, cryptographic discretion-reduction) and traces trajectories through case studies including the English commons, CHI conference capture, Chromium's market dominance, DNS/ICANN's state transition, Linux's 30-year drift toward center, and Ethereum's tensions around Bitmine's treasury strategy and EF succession.</br>Van Epps introduces concepts like 'capture,' 'tenseity' (borrowed from tensional integrity sculptures), and ossification to describe how competing vectors pull a protocol's governance toward different corners over time, closing with open questions about whether AI and post-quantum transitions will shift Ethereum's position in this political economy."
